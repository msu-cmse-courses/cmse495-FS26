# Team Charter  
## CMSE / Data Science / Software Engineering Projects

### Purpose

The Team Charter defines how your team will work together.

It is a living document that evolves as your team grows and the project changes.

A strong charter helps your team:
- communicate clearly  
- build trust  
- anticipate challenges and agree on how to handle them before they occur  

---

## Core Principle

Successful teams do two things:

    solve the problem and build the working relationship

Your charter is created during the “forming” stage.

Its purpose is to prepare you for the “storming” stage, when challenges and miscommunication occur.

When that happens, your charter gives you a shared reference point:

    what we agreed would happen (expectation)  
    what is actually happening (observation)  

---

## What to Include

### 1. Team Members and Roles
List all team members and their roles.

---

### 2. Team Purpose and Goals
What are you trying to accomplish and what does success look like?

---

### 3. Expected Outcomes
What are your current expectations for deliverables?

---

### 4. Communication Norms

Define how your team will communicate:

- tools and channels  
- response expectations  
- how updates are shared  

Include:
- communicate early when something changes  
- raise concerns before they become problems  

---

### 5. Ground Rules (Team Mantras)

This is the most important part of your charter.

Create a small set of clear, memorable agreements that define how your team will respond when challenges arise.

For each rule, think about:

- What is the expectation?
- What will we do if it is not met?

Focus on situations such as:
- someone is late or misses a meeting  
- work is not completed on time  
- unclear expectations  
- disagreements or frustration  

These rules should allow your team to say later:

    “We agreed that we would…”
    “Here is what I observed…”

The goal is to make it easier to communicate and resolve problems without making them personal.

---

## Using Your Charter

- Review and update it regularly  
- Use it when problems arise  
- Adjust it as your team and project evolve  

Your charter is something you point to when things get difficult.
