# Project Expectations Guide  
## CMSE / Data Science / Software Engineering Projects

### Core Principle

Projects are not pre-defined problems with known answers.

Your role is not just to complete work, but to help define, clarify, and refine the problem over time.

Successful teams do two things:
    solve the problem and build the working relationships that make the work possible.

---

## Work in Uncertainty

Projects will vary. Some will be well defined, others will not.

In many cases:
- goals will evolve  
- requirements will change  
- partners may not fully know what they need at the start  

This is not a flaw in the project. It is how real-world work operates.

You will not fully understand the problem at the beginning.  
Understanding develops through exploration and communication.

---

## Start Early, Learn by Doing

Waiting for clarity before taking action does not work.

Instead:

- begin exploring the data early  
- try small analyses or prototypes  
- share what you are seeing  

Understanding comes from interacting with the problem, not from planning alone.

---

## Use Communication to Discover the Problem

Communication is how the problem becomes clear.

Teams should:

- describe their understanding in their own words  
- paraphrase what they think the problem is  
- share early results and interpretations  

This allows instructors and partners to:

- identify misunderstandings  
- correct expectations  
- guide the next step early  

You may not understand what you do not understand yet. Communication helps surface that.

---

## Milestones as Communication Checkpoints

Project milestones are not busy work.  
They are designed to help teams, instructors, and partners stay aligned.

The goal is not just to produce something, but to communicate your current understanding.

Use milestones to:

- explain what you think the problem is  
- describe your approach in your own words  
- show what you tried and what you observed  

This helps others see how you are thinking, not just what you produced.

(For example, a project proposal or early exploratory work is less about being “right” and more about showing your current understanding.)

---

## Using Tools and AI

Tools, including generative AI, can be useful.

However, they can also hide misunderstanding.

You are responsible for:

- understanding what you submit  
- being able to explain it in your own words  
- questioning outputs that you do not fully understand  

If something looks correct but you cannot explain it, communication breaks down.

The goal is not polished output. The goal is shared understanding.

---

## Iterate and Refine

Projects move forward through iteration:

- explore the data  
- form an interpretation  
- communicate your thinking  
- adjust based on feedback  

Progress is not linear. Revising your approach is part of the work.

---

## Bottom Line

In structured assignments, the problem is defined for you.

In projects, defining the problem is part of the work.

Successful teams:
- start early  
- communicate often  
- share incomplete ideas  
- and adjust as they learn
