# Team Charter

The Team Charter is your team's living operating agreement. It documents how your team will work together, make decisions, communicate, and address challenges throughout the semester.

## Purpose

A strong charter helps teams:

- Communicate clearly and consistently.
- Define shared expectations and responsibilities.
- Establish processes for decision making and accountability.
- Address conflicts early using agreed-upon norms.
- Build trust and create a productive team environment.

## Core Principle

Successful teams do not happen by accident. They are built through intentional communication, mutual respect, shared responsibility, and continuous reflection. The Team Charter provides a framework for these practices and should be referenced and updated as the team's needs evolve.

## Living Document

Your charter is not a one-time assignment. It should be reviewed periodically and updated whenever the team identifies new challenges, changes in membership, or opportunities to improve how the team operates.

All team members should contribute to the charter and agree to its contents. By creating and maintaining your charter, you are establishing the expectations that will guide your work throughout the project.

## What a Team Charter Includes

While every team's charter will be different, most effective charters address the following topics:

- Team goals and shared expectations.
- Member roles, responsibilities, and areas of expertise.
- Communication methods and expected response times.
- Meeting schedules, attendance expectations, and participation norms.
- Decision-making processes.
- Processes for tracking work and meeting deadlines.
- Procedures for resolving disagreements, addressing concerns, and requesting support.
- Expectations for professionalism, respect, and accountability.

The charter should provide enough detail that both team members and instructors can understand how the team is organized and how work is coordinated.

## Why Instructors Review Team Charters

Team charters help instructors understand how each team plans to operate. They provide insight into team structure, communication practices, decision-making processes, and individual responsibilities.

When challenges arise, the charter serves as a useful reference point for both the team and instructional staff. A well-maintained charter can help identify gaps in expectations, clarify responsibilities, and support productive conversations about team dynamics and project management.

## Using the Charter Throughout the Semester

Teams should revisit their charter regularly, especially after major project milestones or when recurring challenges emerge.

A charter is most valuable when it reflects how the team actually works. Updating the document as the team grows and adapts helps maintain clear expectations and supports long-term success.

### Team Charter

Create a word document, give it a clear name and add it to your Team directory. This will be a "living" document which means it will change as your team grows.  Remember that we drafted this document in class on Wednesday. To start make sure it includes the following:

* Members and roles
* Team Purpose and goals
* Expected outcomes
* Ground rules
* Instructions/URL for accessing the git repository (See below).
