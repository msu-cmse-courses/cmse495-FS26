# Meeting Agenda and Notes

Effective meetings are a critical part of successful teamwork. This course emphasizes using **a single, evolving document** to organize meeting agendas, capture notes, and track progress over time.

---

## Core Idea

Each team should maintain **one shared document** where:

- The **next or most recent meeting** is always at the top  
- Older meetings are kept below as a running record  
- Agendas and notes are kept together  

This creates a living document that reflects the team’s progress throughout the semester.

---

## Where to Store Meeting Notes

Meeting notes should be stored in your team’s **Microsoft Teams folder**.

- This allows instructors to follow your team’s progress  
- Provides visibility into how your team is working  
- Serves as a written record if challenges or issues arise  

This document is part of your team’s professional workflow and project documentation.

---

## Meeting Structure

Each meeting entry should include the following:

### Basic Information
- **Date and time**
- **Location (or virtual platform)**
- **Attendees (who was present)**

### Roles
Each meeting should assign the following roles:

- **Facilitator** – guides the discussion and helps ensure the meeting stays focused and productive  
- **Timekeeper** – responsible for coordinating the agenda before the meeting and managing time and flow during the meeting  
- **Note Taker** – records key discussion points and action items  

---

## Agenda

The agenda should be written **before the meeting** and include:

- Key topics to discuss  
- Decisions that need to be made  
- Updates or issues to review  

A clear agenda helps ensure meetings are focused and productive.

---

## Notes

Meeting notes should be:

- **Concise** (not word-for-word transcripts)  
- Focused on **key ideas and decisions**  
- Organized so they are easy to review later  

Notes should capture:

- Important discussion points  
- Decisions made  
- Open questions  

---

## Action Items

Every meeting should clearly identify:

- **What needs to be done**
- **Who is responsible**
- **When it will be completed**

Action items should be easy to scan and unambiguous.

---

## Sharing Expectations

- Notes from meetings with **community partners** should be shared with those partners  
- Notes from **internal team meetings** should remain within the team (and instructors via Teams)  

This ensures appropriate transparency with partners while maintaining internal team coordination.

---

## Example Format

```
## Meeting: Week 3 Check-in

**Date:** Sept 18  
**Time:** 1:00–2:00 PM  
**Location:** Zoom  
**Attendees:** Riley, Jordan, Casey  

**Roles**
- Facilitator: Riley  
- Timekeeper: Jordan  
- Note Taker: Casey  

### Agenda
- Review last week’s progress  
- Discuss dataset challenges  
- Plan next milestone  

### Notes
- Data cleaning is taking longer than expected due to missing values  
- Decided to simplify the initial model to focus on baseline results  
- Need clarification from community partner on data definitions  

### Action Items
- Riley → clean dataset subset by Wednesday  
- Jordan → build baseline model by Friday  
- Casey → email community partner questions by Tuesday  
```

---

## Expectations

- Teams should update this document regularly  
- The most recent meeting should always appear at the top  
- This document should reflect **ongoing communication and progress**  

This document serves as both:

- a coordination tool for the team  
- a record of project development over time  

---

## Why This Matters

Well-structured meetings help teams:

- stay organized  
- communicate effectively  
- identify and resolve issues early  

Clear agendas and notes are a key part of **professional teamwork**.
