# MILESTONE: Project Exploration and Plan

![Graphic of a Gantt Chart for project planning](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/aa/Project_schedule.png?20160608211101)

[Image from wikipidia](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Project_schedule.png)

## Purpose

Before developing software, building models, conducting analyses, or proposing technical solutions, teams should first develop a clear understanding of the project resources available to them.

The purpose of this milestone is to explore the project's available data, information sources, software, documents, images, simulations, workflows, or other resources and use those observations to develop an informed project plan for the remainder of the semester.

Projects vary considerably. Some teams may have immediate access to large datasets while others may still be obtaining data, approvals, software access, or project materials. Regardless of project status, every team should demonstrate meaningful exploration of the resources currently available.

This report is intended to serve as the foundation for future project documentation. The exploratory analysis, figures, tables, observations, and planning developed for this assignment should be refined and expanded throughout the semester rather than recreated from scratch. Content from this report may eventually appear in project presentations, progress reports, technical documentation, websites, posters, and the final report.


## Deliverables

Submit a single report containing the following sections.

### 1. Project Overview

Provide a brief introduction to the project.

Include:

- Project title
- Team members
- Community partner or stakeholder
- Problem being addressed
- Expected project outcomes

The overview should help a reader quickly understand the purpose and scope of the project.

### 2. Project Exploration

Explore and document the resources available to your team.

Depending on the project, this may include:

- Datasets
- Images
- Documents
- Databases
- Simulations
- Existing software
- Research literature
- Workflows
- Survey responses
- Sensor data
- Laboratory measurements
- Other project-specific resources

Summarize what you learned during your exploration.

When appropriate, include:

- Data size and quantity
- Data structure and organization
- Examples of records, files, samples, or outputs
- Summary statistics
- Tables
- Graphs
- Charts
- Visualizations
- Screenshots
- Initial patterns or observations
- Data quality concerns
- Missing information
- Questions raised during exploration

The goal is not to perform a complete analysis. The goal is to develop an informed understanding of your project's starting point.

Teams should create professional figures and summaries with the expectation that they may be reused and expanded in future course deliverables.

### 3. Success Criteria and Evaluation

Describe how your team will determine whether the project is making meaningful progress and how you will evaluate the success of the final solution.

Success criteria should be connected to project goals, stakeholder needs, and the expected project outcomes described in the Project Overview.

Success may be measured in many ways depending on the project. Examples include:

- Model performance metrics
- Processing speed or efficiency improvements
- Accuracy or reliability of results
- Data quality improvements
- Successful completion of required features
- User satisfaction or usability improvements
- Successful integration into an existing workflow
- Reproducibility of results
- Stakeholder acceptance of a proposed solution

For each success criterion, describe:

- What will be measured
- Why it matters
- How it will be evaluated
- What outcome would be considered successful

Not all success criteria must be fully defined at this stage. If important evaluation methods or performance targets are still uncertain, identify them as open loops and include a plan for refining them during the semester.

The goal is to move beyond simply completing tasks and begin defining how your team will recognize meaningful progress and project success.

### 4. Project Plan

Using the information learned during project exploration, describe your proposed approach for the remainder of the semester.

Include:

- Major project goals
- Expected milestones
- Team responsibilities
- Proposed tools and technologies
- Planned workflows
- Deliverables
- Preliminary timeline

Your plan should reflect what was learned during the exploration phase. Explain how your understanding of the available resources influenced your proposed approach.

The project plan should also describe how the team intends to make progress toward the success criteria defined above. Consider both immediate tasks and longer-term milestones that will help move the project toward a successful outcome.

### 5. Open Loops

Identify important questions, uncertainties, assumptions, or unresolved issues that could affect the project.

An "open loop" is something your team currently does not fully understand or cannot yet answer, but will need to address during the semester.

Examples may include:

- Defining or refining success criteria
- Access to data or project resources
- Unknown data quality issues
- Uncertain technical approaches
- Missing stakeholder requirements
- Software or hardware limitations
- Questions about project scope
- Model performance or evaluation methods
- Regulatory or compliance concerns

For each open loop, describe:

- What is currently unknown
- Why it matters to the project
- How the team plans to investigate or resolve it
- A target milestone or timeframe for closing the loop

Teams are not expected to have every answer at this stage. However, teams should demonstrate awareness of important uncertainties and provide a realistic strategy for reducing them over time.

A successful project balances progress and discovery. Teams should avoid postponing important open loops until late in the semester, but should also avoid focusing exclusively on unresolved questions while neglecting tasks that are already well understood.

## Effective Use of AI

AI tools may be used as part of this assignment. However, the strongest reports are those that clearly communicate project-specific observations, decisions, and plans.

Use AI to help improve organization, writing, coding, and analysis. Do not use AI to replace project exploration or critical thinking.

Focus on evidence rather than volume. Figures, tables, screenshots, examples, observations, and project-specific insights are more valuable than lengthy narrative text. Reports that are repetitive, generic, or disconnected from the team's actual exploration may receive lower scores regardless of writing quality.

## Looking Ahead

This milestone is intended to become part of your project's living documentation.

As the semester progresses, teams are expected to revise, improve, and expand this work. Material developed for this report may later be incorporated into:

- Progress reports
- Project presentations
- Project websites
- Technical documentation
- Final reports
- Appendices and supporting materials

Strong reports will be written with future reuse in mind. Clear explanations, well-designed visualizations, and thoughtful documentation created now can save substantial effort later in the semester.

## Submission

1. Save your report in your team's private milestone folder. Give it a clear name based on the date:

   `YYYYMMDD-TeamName-Exploration-and-Plan.docx`

2. Convert a non-editable copy of your report to PDF.
3. Upload the PDF to the public team folder in Microsoft Teams by the assignment deadline.
4. Be prepared to discuss and provide feedback on other teams' reports during the review period.
5. After receiving feedback from instructors and classmates, revise your report as needed.
6. Compose a professional email to your community partner that summarizes your progress and includes the revised PDF report as an attachment. Carbon Copy (CC) all members of your team (including yourself) and CC the course instructors.

The goal of sharing the report with your community partner is to communicate your current understanding of the project, gather feedback, and confirm that your team's direction aligns with stakeholder expectations.

## Evaluation

Reports will be evaluated holistically based on:

- Evidence of meaningful project exploration
- Quality of success criteria and evaluation measures
- Realism and completeness of the project plan
- Identification and management of open loops
- Professional communication and documentation
- Evidence that planning is informed by exploration and project goals

Teams are not expected to have all the answers at this stage. Strong teams demonstrate an understanding of what they know, what they do not yet know, and how they plan to move forward.

## Common Feedback from Previous Teams

- Explore available resources before committing to a technical solution.
- Use existing tools and libraries whenever practical.
- Your plan should focus on making a simple end-to-end workflow quickly and then improve it iteratively.
- Establish baseline results before pursuing advanced methods.
- Anticipate project risks early.
- Communicate frequently with teammates and stakeholders.
- Document decisions as they are made.
- Create figures, tables, and summaries that can be reused throughout the semester.
